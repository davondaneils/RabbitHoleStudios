# The Setup — character visual style guide (v1)
**Direction selected:** 2026-10-13. **Scope:** the entire *The Setup* true-story animated anthology, not just the Oobah episode.
**Visual target LOCKED; individual assets NOT automatically approved.** The user selected polished mature 2D TV animation broadly inspired by *Archer*. What matters is its **economy of drawing**, not higher illustration detail or copying existing characters.

## Identity vs. style: never confuse them
- **Style references** define the rendering technique, line economy, proportions and shading — **not** the characters' identities. Reuse the supplied TV-frame screenshots.
- **Identity references** are genuine photographs of the *specific real person* being depicted, ideally near the story's historical period. Their haircut, hairline, facial geometry and distinctive asymmetries take priority over any generic animation archetype.
- Every case gets its **own real-person-based cast**, not a recurring on-screen host, narrator avatar, animal mascot or roster of invented “types.” Narration is off-screen.

## What to reproduce visually
| Dimension | Required look |
|---|---|
| Silhouette and anatomy | Adult, believable proportions and recognizably individual body/head shapes; no heroic template. |
| Outlines | Smooth, clean, confidently dark/black external outlines; fewer lighter/simpler interior marks for eyes, nose, jaw, mouth and clothes. |
| Color | Matte **flat fills** with clear shape boundaries. Muted/grounded palette is a starting point, not a rigid costume/skin color palette. |
| Shadows | A few **large, crisp, hard-edged cel-shadow polygons**; typically 1–2 per visible facial plane. No complex multi-layer rendering. |
| Face | Recognizable likeness with deliberately **sparse** internal lines. Preserve real nose, brows, eye shape/spacing, cheeks, chin, hairline. |
| Hair | Large coherent graphic shapes and a few selective highlight regions; preserve the person's **actual cut and silhouette**, not detailed strands. |
| Expressions | Subtle, clear, human and slightly dry, rather than exaggerated emoji/cartoon faces. |
| Clothing | Natural everyday folds simplified into large shadow regions. Clothes reflect the documented person's context, not default spy suits. |
| Backgrounds | Believable locations with more structural detail than the characters when needed; don't make faces painterly to match them. |
| Framing | Television-ready cinematic angles; consistently the same drawing grammar from full body to close-up. |

**Central lesson from the iterations:** “more polished” does **not** mean more tiny lines, gradients, realism or editorial/character concept-art effects. The polished result comes from drawing discipline. If in doubt, **subtract detail**.

## Avoid / reject
Painterly textures, airbrushing, gradients, skin pores, individual hair strands, hatching, glossy 3D lighting, intricate cheek shading, superhero jaws/brows, perfectly groomed leading-man hair, anime proportions, photorealistic rendering, “generic AI cartoon,” copying Archer faces/costumes/frames, composite mismatched characters across views. Do not make an original character into “Sterling Archer with a wig.”

## QA gate — required for EACH new character
1. **Recognizability:** Could someone familiar with the real person identify them without reading the name? Check actual hair shape, hairline, face, eyes, nose and jaw; don't invent certainty if references are insufficient.
2. **Frame-level style:** Compare beside the exact supplied style screenshots. Strong black outline? Flat fills? Few hard-edged shadow shapes? NO microdetail? Reject if it resembles concept art more than a TV animation frame.
3. **Cross-view continuity:** Same face, haircut, age, body, outfit and palette from front to 3/4 to true side and expression closeups. No doubled front view posing as a 3/4.
4. **Production viability:** Could we recreate that character in several shots at sensible cost and edit effort? Keep the founder time/credit count.
5. **Status:** Only tag as **APPROVED / LOCKED CHARACTER** after explicit visual acceptance. “Close” or “much better” = **candidate**.
