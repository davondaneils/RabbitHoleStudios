# PSY-A-01 — The Setup: The fake No. 1 restaurant

**Status:** Story premise approved; research, script and footage not approved. **Format:** Short sourced deception reconstruction. Length TBD by beat sheet (consider 45–90 seconds).

**Audience experience:** Confidence (“I'd spot the scam”) → disbelief → recognition → useful insight. The real threat is fabricated reputation becoming apparently credible.

**Audience-value gate (working hypothesis; not script approval):** This story belongs in the Psychology / Power / Influence account because the intended viewer wants to spot manipulation, avoid looking foolish and understand the social mechanisms behind apparently trustworthy signals. The *entertainment* is the audacity and escalating absurdity of the fake restaurant. The *reward beyond amusement* is recognizing that fabricated reviews, a prestigious-looking ranking and apparent exclusivity can reinforce one another while perhaps all tracing back to the same manipulated reputation—**not necessarily independent proof of quality**. The *practical recognition* is to investigate where signals of credibility originate. This is a reasonable interpretive angle to substantiate from the record, **not** a proven universal psychological law. Buyer overlap with paid persuasion training remains unvalidated; an interactive credibility/scam-spotting experience may be a more natural test.

**Possible narrative payoff to investigate, not yet verified or approved:** The staged one-night dinner may produce a stronger ending than merely reaching #1 because it tests how initial expectations interact with actual experience. Check original accounts and independent reporting for guests' different reactions; do not claim everyone wanted to return.

**Promise:** Oobah Butler made a nonexistent London restaurant top-ranked on TripAdvisor, prompting real people to seek reservations.

**Possible opening, NOT final copy:** A “No. 1 in London” restaurant listing cut against a plain garden shed. “London's best restaurant was a shed.”

**Story spine:** Fake reviews and fabricated scarcity → apparent legitimacy → ranking climbs → people seek bookings → reveal the restaurant never functioned as a normal business → why social proof and authority cues worked.
