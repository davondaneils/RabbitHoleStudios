# PSY-B-01 — The Human Experiment: The decoy that changes your choice

**Status:** Premise approved; demonstration and script unfinished.

**Core pull:** “I understand why I choose things” → confident decision → unexpected reinterpretation → improved judgment.

**Promise:** Show a viewer three subscription offers, invite a choice, remove an apparently pointless option, and demonstrate how context affects the relative appeal of remaining offers.

**Opening:** Three legible subscription cards and a direct “Which would you buy?” question. Do not claim viewers actually voted or report invented response percentages.

**Beat line:** Present options → viewer mentally commits → identify asymmetrically dominated decoy → remove it/compare options → explain the decoy effect and limits → practical takeaway on judging underlying value.

**Research:** Dan Ariely, *Predictably Irrational* (Economist subscription example); evaluate peer-reviewed context and limitations. Research lead: https://www.nature.com/articles/palcomms201682

**Truth standard:** This is an illustrative interaction, NOT data collected from Reel viewers. Decoy effects vary by context.

**Assets:** Pricing cards, option highlights, countdown/pause, few expressive character or narrator shots, clean transition. Reusable for other honest interactive psychological exercises.

**Commercial hypothesis:** Interactive choice architecture and persuasion scenario pack; unvalidated.

**Second execution:** Distinct researched human decision phenomenon, TBD.
