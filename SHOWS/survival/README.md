# Account mini-bible: Survival & Preparedness

**Updated:** 2026-10-15. **Stage:** POSITIONING / PILOT DEVELOPMENT.
**Status:** Founder-approved working direction: practical household readiness, with blackouts as the starting focus. Exact audience, geography, buyer demand, product and economics remain unvalidated. This is not script, asset or publication approval.
**Basis:** Founder-supplied Survival Account Positioning and Content Handoff and the subsequent approved positioning discussion, 2026-10-15. This supersedes the earlier broad account emphasis; existing pilot premises remain available.

## Account promise

**“We show what happens when everyday systems fail—and help you prepare your household before you need to.”**

The account turns uncertainty into capability. Viewers should leave a story thinking: “I hadn't considered that—and now I know what to check.” This is proposed audience recognition, not a sourced customer quote.

**Starting focus:** Household blackouts and the everyday dependencies an outage exposes. Broader preparedness is a possible later expansion, informed by audience evidence. Energy dependence and current events can supply context and curiosity; practical household readiness is the reason to follow.

## Working audience and emotional job

**Starting audience hypothesis:** People responsible for a household who feel underprepared and do not know what to prioritize. Do not narrow this automatically to battery shoppers, homeowners, fathers, US residents or experienced preppers.

The practical job is to understand what their household depends on, decide what needs attention first, and make a plan they can use. The emotional job is to regain a justified sense of control and safety and feel capable of looking after the people who depend on them.

Motivations to investigate include responsibility, independence, affordability, fear of being caught unprepared, frustration with scattered advice, and pride in being dependable. Anxiety is a starting concern; increasing fear is not the account's promised reward. Buyer interest in organized planning remains a hypothesis distinct from interest in dramatic scenarios.

**Unresolved:** Country/local hazards, renter/homeowner and apartment/house split, household composition, budget, existing readiness and willingness to pay. A high-rise household and a rural household need different plans. Choose and research a first segment before presenting advice or a product as tailored to it.

## Positioning, story angle and tone

Practical preparedness told with the curiosity and clarity of an investigative explainer. Use this working progression, adapting it to the story:

**Interesting dependency → clearly framed disruption → household consequences or decision → useful response.**

Each episode resolves one practical question. The entertainment reveals an overlooked dependency or consequential choice; the payoff supplies a useful check or safe, sourced action. A merely alarming scenario is insufficient. Apply the [writing library](../../STUDIO/writing/README.md): familiar words, connected narration, clear cause and effect, and an ending with enough time for the meaning to land.

**Content pillars:**
- Hidden dependencies: what an outage affects beyond visible lighting.
- Scenario stories: a household faces a defined interruption and must prioritize.
- Escalating consequences: show how a longer disruption changes the decision, with assumptions stated.
- Preparedness mistakes: supplies that are inaccessible, missing coordination or a plan nobody knows.
- Practical priorities: what to assess, prepare or discuss first, suited to the chosen household context.
- Claims examined: investigate a surprising vulnerability or controversial assertion and resolve it with evidence and a household implication.

**Voice:** Curious, vivid, calm, skeptical and practical. Specific objects, constraints and decisions carry tension. Equipment supports a plan; it is not the whole account. Do not build the positioning around conspiracy identity. Investigative curiosity can be tested without implying unsupported plots or presenting hypothetical footage as news.

## Commercial direction — hypothesis to validate

**Preferred model:** B2C self-serve information product, automated delivery and limited routine fulfillment. Individual consulting, installer lead generation and a battery-buying-only guide are outside the current direction.

**Working product:** A household blackout planning guide that helps buyers produce their own usable plan. Organize it around before an outage, during it, and reassessing if it continues. Possible components: needs assessment, purchase priorities, household worksheets, printable instructions, checklists, short lessons and worked scenarios.

The reason to pay must be usable organization and decisions for the buyer's situation, beyond collecting facts already available free. This differentiation is proposed, not established customer demand. Exact scope, expert review, pricing, maintenance and conversion economics are unresolved.

**Possible path to test:** Useful story → readiness check/sample → honest product preview → paid guide. This is not an implemented or validated funnel. Optional scenario packs or disclosed affiliate recommendations may fit later if they add value; do not assume an existing offer, price or sales result. No safety-critical instruction is withheld to force a purchase.

## Evidence and next decisions

Verify local safety advice with current credible sources before scripting. Distinguish a hypothetical outage from a forecast. Oil supply, fuel costs, electricity reliability and local outage risk require separate causal evidence; a global energy headline alone is not a local blackout prediction.

Do not use reserves-to-production ratios as exhaustion countdowns. No invented tests, experts, testimonials, guaranteed protection or fake shortages. The overall impression of a scenario must be honest as well as its labels. These boundaries support audience trust and useful decisions.

**Next research:** Select geography and a first household context; find authentic audience language and priority problems; compare existing free guidance with what people struggle to apply; test demand for planning help before building an extensive product. Competitor offers are research leads, not proof of our buyer economics. See the [research register](../../RESEARCH/source-register.md#survival-positioning-research-queue--2026-10-15).
