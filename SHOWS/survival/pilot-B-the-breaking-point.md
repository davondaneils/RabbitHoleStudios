# SUR-B-01 — The Breaking Point: Floor 20

**Status:** Premise approved; fictional serial drama, not a reported incident. Local safety research needed.

**Positioning fit (2026-10-15):** Lead survival development candidate under the blackout-first [account direction](README.md). Show how a household dependency becomes a practical decision and resolve one useful question. The apartment setting is a story premise, not a validated audience choice; geography and household fit remain open. Begin with audience/source research, not automatic scripting or production.

**Core pull:** “What happens when I discover the essentials weren't where I needed them?” A reliable family leader faces a plausible, cascading crisis.

**Scene:** Family on the 20th floor loses electricity and water pressure. Elevator stops. Some emergency supplies are in the parked car. An older neighbor asks for help. Every choice affects the next constraint.

**Possible hook:** “Twenty floors up. No elevator. No running water. And the emergency kit's in the trunk.”

**Required local payoff:** The family identifies one overlooked dependency, resolves an immediate problem safely, and faces a new consequence. Avoid an empty “Part 2” cliffhanger.

**Research lead:** Public Safety Canada outage guidance: https://www.getprepared.gc.ca/cnt/rsrcs/pblctns/pwrtgs-wtd/index-en.aspx

**Safety:** Don't assume all power outages stop water automatically; establish building circumstances. No indoor generator misuse, unsafe evacuation, obstruction of stairwells or unverified medical advice.

**Assets:** Modular apartment building cutaway, 20th floor, hall, stairwell, tap, emergency bag in car, neighbor, family cast, status indicators. Reuse settings.

**Commercial hypothesis:** A self-serve blackout planning guide could help someone assess their own dependencies and create household instructions. High-rise worksheets or scenario packs may be useful variants if that segment is selected and validated. Product scope, price and buyer demand remain unresolved.
