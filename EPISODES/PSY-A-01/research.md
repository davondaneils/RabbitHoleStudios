## The best concrete story material
- **Opening physical contrast:** Burner phone vibrating amid shed/small domestic surroundings while strangers request reservations for a prestigious-sounding restaurant.
- **Food-reveal gag:** Fine-dining egg photograph flips to reveal the photographer's bare foot beneath it; another dish is a sponge and shaving foam.
- **Scarcity:** Only the road, no number; “appointment only” sounds like rarefied access, but avoids unexpected visitors. Callers told it is fully booked.
- **Visible stakes/escalation:** Ranking from #18,149 toward #1; email and call traffic; job applicants and suppliers; a PR firm offers to promote a restaurant that isn't open.
- **Payoff:** To sustain the hoax he opens for one night. Actual diners, actors voicing delight, cheap ready meals, DJ-made crowd/restaurant sounds; feedback ranges from apparent dissatisfaction to requests to book again.
- **Psychological payoff shown visually:** Fake reviews create rank; the rank attracts genuine attention; withholding reservations suggests exclusivity; at dinner, the staged crowd is another cue. The cues look additive, but are not four independent checks of culinary quality.

## What should *not* be in the episode without additional sourcing
- “Everyone loved it,” “every guest asked to return,” “customers paid £xxx,” “there were zero dinners ever,” or exact confirmed date/duration of #1.
- Direct fabricated dialogue, invented private thoughts Oobah never expressed, or official-looking recreations of real TripAdvisor interface presented as genuine 2017 archival footage.
- A strong scientific claim that *social proof* or *scarcity* has been proven to cause any specific diner's behavior in this case; this was a stunt with confounds, not a controlled study.
- Screenshots, music, photos, trademarks or actual voices reused without legal/licensing clearance. **VICE itself noted that TripAdvisor screenshots in the article were mocked up on legal advice**, so label any recreations.
