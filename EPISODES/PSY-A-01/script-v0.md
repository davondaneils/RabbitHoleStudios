# PSY-A-01 — Full episode draft v0 (pre-editorial)
**Status:** superseded internal draft, preserved to make editorial changes inspectable. Written after audience gate and full story treatment, not approved or production copy.

## Voiceover draft
Somebody calls a London restaurant and asks for a table.
The man on the phone says they're booked for six weeks.
He leaves out one small detail.
The restaurant is a garden shed.

It's 2017. Writer Oobah Butler has an unusual qualification: he used to get paid ten pounds to write glowing TripAdvisor reviews for restaurants he'd never visited.
So he asks himself: what if the restaurant were fake too?

He buys a ten-pound phone, builds a website, and gives the place a name: The Shed at Dulwich.
No street number. Just appointment only.
Very exclusive. Also very convenient when your restaurant doesn't exist.

His menu is organized around moods.
The photos look like fine dining, until you notice one features an egg resting on his foot. Another is a painted sponge with shaving foam.

TripAdvisor approves the listing. Oobah asks friends to post rave reviews.
He starts dead last: number 18,149.

Then his phone rings.

A stranger wants dinner. Oobah panics and says they're booked for six weeks.
More people call. He keeps turning them away.
Soon suppliers want his business. People apply for jobs. A PR agency offers to represent him.

And eventually, The Shed reaches number one on TripAdvisor in London.

Now he has a problem. People genuinely want to eat there.

So he opens it. One night only.

Some guests arrive blindfolded. A DJ plays restaurant noises in the garden. Actors at the other tables gush about their meals.
The food? Dressed-up microwave ready meals.

Not everybody's convinced. One couple leaves unimpressed.
But another table asks when they can book again.

And that's the strange part.
The reviews helped create the ranking. The ranking made the restaurant look important. Every fully booked phone call made it feel harder to get into. Even the delighted people at dinner were partly staged.

Reviews. Ranking. Scarcity. Buzz. Happy diners.
Five convincing signals.
Follow them backward, though, and you arrive at the same bloke with a burner phone.

And a shed.
