## Story approaches and hook decision
- **Impossible result:** A garden shed reached #1 before serving a meal. Selected for clear immediate orientation and a specific contradiction; the review-origin question gives it this account's purpose.
- **Reality catches up:** Start with guests eating ready meals in a garden, then explain the restaurant's manufactured reputation. More immediate physical scene, but requires a backward time shift.
- **Viewer trust check:** Start with checking reviews and rankings that share a source. Closest to the audience job, but more abstract before the shed establishes the problem.

These alternatives are test hypotheses, not ranked by measured performance. The selected version delivers the whole story and can stand alone.

## Judgment of the finished script
The opening promises an explanation of fabricated reputation. The listing/review sequence answers part of that question; genuine demand changes the consequence; dinner introduces real experience alongside more staged praise; the final dependency explanation pays off the original review question. The birthday inquiry retains a specific human decision without invented emotions.

The audience reward is recognition that several credibility cues need not be independent. This fits the account's working discernment promise. It does not establish a universal bias, accuse actual diners of stupidity or advertise an unvalidated product.

The independent editor confirmed that both substantive repairs were applied and found no further material text issue requiring a rewrite. This is an editorial judgment, not certification of attention, comprehension or conversion.

## Remaining tradeoffs and testing hypothesis
At 203 words, the draft loses some visual comedy and contextual detail from V4. The mechanism paragraph still asks the listener to connect review and ranking; a clear visual connection could help after approval, but the narration states the relationship directly. Different sources may still be wrong, so the final question is a starting check rather than a foolproof test.
