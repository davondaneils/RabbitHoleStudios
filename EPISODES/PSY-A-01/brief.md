## Mandatory audience-value gate
- **Viewer (hypothesis):** Someone who enjoys spotting deception and hidden influence, hates the possibility of being manipulated or publicly embarrassed, and wants to feel perceptive and socially sharp. They are not necessarily buyers of persuasion courses.
- **Why THIS story belongs in THIS account:** It shows the origin and reinforcement of reputational signals in an actual documented hoax: made-up reviews → legitimizing rank → manufactured exclusivity → genuine demand → staged social reassurance at dinner. A random eccentric-restaurant anecdote would lack that precise demonstration.
- **Entertainment reward:** Audacity and escalating disbelief: a fake restaurant gets official listing status, attracts real callers and PR interest, reaches #1, then serves one-pound microwave meals to real guests.
- **Deeper reward:** The viewer spots that multiple plausible credibility cues can be descendants of a **single manipulated source** and aren't automatically independent evidence. Recognition in reviews, “fully booked,” apparent institutional endorsement, online popularity and expert popularity.
- **What evidence does/doesn't establish:** Butler's account and contemporary reporting support the chronology of the stunt. They **do not prove** that a single universal bias caused every caller's or diner's behavior. Some guests were unimpressed; others reportedly asked to return; dinner was free and actors influenced atmosphere.
- **Plausible paid adjacency, unvalidated:** Interactive credibility-checking/deception detection cases might fit better than general negotiation training. Audience/buyer overlap remains to be tested, not assumed.
- **Gate owner:** founder; **not yet explicitly approved**.

## Dramatic promise / engine
**One-sentence promise:** A writer made Londoners chase reservations at a nonexistent restaurant; the episode reveals how a handful of fabricated signals can snowball into apparently independent reasons to believe something is desirable.

**Question:** Can the reputation of a place get so far ahead of reality that people keep buying into it **even after they finally see the place**?

**What the viewer first assumes:** A top-ranked, hard-to-book London restaurant must have served great meals to real customers.

**Escalating corrections:** No real restaurant → fake food pictures → friends' manufactured reviews → TripAdvisor listing/rank → real calls and apparent scarcity → real suppliers/PR attention → number one → one-night dinner → mixed reactions, including return requests.

**Payoff:** What looked like numerous endorsements was, at least initially, a chain of dependent signals engineered around a garden shed. Then dinner adds a more ambiguous human twist: some guests were not convinced and some reportedly wanted another booking.

**Emotional motion:** Curiosity → delighted disbelief → anticipation/astonishment → tension (“what happens when people actually arrive?”) → conflicted surprise → recognition.

## Scope and medium
- **Format:** One complete off-screen-narrated true-story episode, rather than a teaser or video part designed to withhold the ending.
- **Candidate length:** ~2:10–2:45, based on ~330 spoken words with purposeful pauses and visual reveals. We chose this only after building the full causal arc, not because it matches a predetermined 60-second quota. Could later be edited shorter if story remains intact.
- **Presentation:** Real Oobah Butler illustrated in original simple adult 2D cel animation, using photo identity references and TV-style drawing grammar as described in `SHOWS/psychology/the-setup-style-guide.md`. No recurring on-screen host. **No detailed storyboard until founder script approval.**
- **Claims:** TripAdvisor #1 is a **site ranking at the time**, not an independent proclamation of restaurant quality. Exact day it reached first place is inconsistent across sources; omit a precise date from narration. One-night dinner actually occurred.
- **Next required approval:** Is the account/audience promise compelling? Is the full story worth watching? If yes, approve or revise `script.md`, then plan animation proof from the finished story.
