# Studio editorial workflow — audience-first, full-story-first
**Status:** Required working process for pilot scripts, established 2026-10-13. Applies to Psychology, Survival and Christian accounts. **This is a routing/gating process, not a demand that every episode use every creative technique.** See [active Creative OS](creative-os.md) for writing standards and [audience research](audience-research.md) for ICP hypotheses.

## Required reading and clarity review

Before each writing task, follow [the writing-library routing table](writing/README.md). Full script creation and substantive revision require all five craft modules. Before presenting the candidate, use [the critical editing pass](writing/editing.md) and fix passages that a distracted listener would need to reconstruct. Record exact problems and repairs rather than scoring the presence of techniques. Audio and visual checks remain separate from text review.

## Principle: the episode serves a viewer job, not just a good anecdote
For *each* episode, establish these five things *before* script drafting:
1. **Specific audience / viewer job** — which account/segment, what do they actively desire or fear, what kind of social/emotional payoff do they seek? Separate confirmed audience evidence from our hypotheses.
2. **Why this story, for this audience** — what meaningful conflict or revelation dramatizes that desire? **What would be lost if we replaced this story with a random entertaining anecdote?**
3. **The promised reward** — beyond curiosity, what *feeling*, insight, recognition, practical judgment or changed perspective should the viewer receive? Do not bolt a generic lesson onto the final sentence.
4. **The actual mechanism and proof** — what documented event, causal chain, behavior or uncertainty embodies that reward? For psychological mechanisms, separate a plausible interpretation from a universal law or established scientific finding.
5. **Commercial adjacency (hypothesis)** — if the viewer loved this, what related problem might they *actually pay* to solve? Differentiate story fans from qualified buyers. No forced sales pitch inside a narrative.

**Stop condition:** If the audience link or promised reward feels generic, repair the premise before researching an entire production or writing a script. If not supported by evidence, label it an *audience hypothesis*.

## Work stages, inputs, deliverables, approval gates

|---|---|---|---|
| 0 — Commission + audience value | Read `README.md`, `STUDIO/decisions.md`, `STUDIO/audience-research.md`, `STUDIO/creative-os.md`, relevant account mini-bible, pilot brief; apply five questions above. | One-page episode brief: viewer, desire, reason this story, promise, emotional motion, potential buyer link | Confirm story earns its place in **this account** |
| 1 — Research | Read originals and corroboration; log interesting true incidents, motivations, turning points, concrete props, sources, disputed facts; mark reenactments vs documented quotes. | Claim/source register + narrative details bank, unknowns and confidence | No invented quotes or unsupported dramatic claims |
| 2 — Story architecture | Develop genuinely different angles and make a **complete** causal beginning-to-ending dramatic treatment; design reveal, escalation, payoff and viewer recognition. | 2–3 differentiated story approaches and recommended complete beat sheet | A full story with satisfying ending, not a 20–30-second fragment |
| 3 — First full script | Draft the whole episode to the length the story earns (not a preset quota). Write what we see/hear and preserve scene-level behavior/humor. | Complete narrated script and story notes | Readable aloud; emotionally and causally coherent |
| 4 — Separate editorial pass | Critique against Creative OS: actual hook contract, changing stakes, specific lived texture, unnatural phrasing, pacing, emotional motion, gratuitous narration, weak/redundant beats and incomplete payoff. | Concrete editor notes + corrected complete script | Rewrites address actual problems; no generic polishing loop |
| 5 — Accuracy + script approval | Source/claim audit, historical qualifications, recognizable portrayal ethics, mobile legibility and full VO read-through. | Versioned **candidate / approved** script and open issues | **Founder approves full episode before shot production** |
| 6 — Storyboard and production proof | Only after script treatment is complete: design shots, motion, sound, style/identity reference pack and production feasibility. | 7 — Finish, release, learn | Assembly, on-phone QA, source and captions audit, publication approval, analytics. | Finished video and `TRACKING/pilots.csv` updates | Evidence informs next episode, not blanket formula |

### Distinguish creative work from mechanical checks
Automated validation can verify that stage artifacts, links, source notes and approvals **exist**. It cannot tell whether a story is riveting, an insight is genuinely earned, or a real person looks accurate. Those require substantive editorial judgment. Don't manufacture quantitative script quality scores.

### Lightweight task context packet
For the relevant account/episode, load only: root `AGENTS.md` + current `STUDIO/decisions.md` + `STUDIO/audience-research.md` + relevant `SHOWS/<account>/README.md` + episode brief + `STUDIO/creative-os.md` + stage-specific files (sources, script, style guides). Use `ARCHIVE/` only when a specific section answers an open question. **Do not blindly dump 50 sections into every prompt.**

At the start of work, **name the files consulted and state the current stage**. If a required file is unavailable, say so; do not proceed as though it was read. Keep explicit output status: `IDEA`, `RESEARCHED`, `TREATMENT`, `SCRIPT_CANDIDATE`, `SCRIPT_APPROVED`, `PRODUCTION`, `PUBLISHED`. Do not imply approvals.

### The Setup pilot: audience-value example (hypothesis, not final script)
- **Viewer:** wants to see hidden persuasion/influence games and avoid being fooled or embarrassed; enjoys feeling perceptive.
- **Narrative question:** how did a fictitious restaurant become convincing enough for strangers to trust it?
- **Mechanism to examine:** fabricated positive reviews, platform ranking, apparent scarcity and social proof can reinforce one another; apparent signals may **not be independent evidence**. This is a defensible interpretive angle, **not proof** that every diner reacted the same way or that everyone falls for it.
- **Viewer reward:** surprise + the satisfying ability to question where signals of credibility originated; concrete recognizability in everyday product/services/expert decisions.
- **Story's ending should be earned:** the one-night staged dinner offers a richer possible payoff than just hitting #1. Verify the documented range of guest reactions rather than declaring all guests were convinced.
- **Audience/buyer gap:** fans of amusing deception stories are *not automatically* purchasers of negotiation/influence training. Treat deception-recognition exercises as an unvalidated closer adjacency.

### Mistake to avoid
A 27-second opening, however catchy, is **not a completed video script** and cannot be treated as the episode's whole dramatic structure. Develop the whole arc and episode script first, then select a section for style/animation proof.
