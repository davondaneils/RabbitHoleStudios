# Story structure, tension and emotional movement
Sources: original Creative OS §§12–19; active `../creative-os.md` and `../editorial-workflow.md`.

Write the complete beginning, middle and ending before polishing individual lines. State one spine: a particular person tries something; events change the situation; the viewer receives a specific payoff. The five-beat example in the archive is illustrative, not a required format or runtime.

For each beat, ask what changes and why it follows. Useful changes include a new obstacle, a consequence of a choice, new evidence, a rising cost, or a reversal of expectation. Replace lists of things that happened with understandable relationships. Use “so,” “because,” “but,” “when,” and “then” where they clarify those relationships; never invent causation to make the story smoother.

Tension requires an understandable unresolved question, not rapid cuts or cryptic narration. A concrete request that cannot be fulfilled can carry more tension than a dramatic claim about everything being at risk. Keep stakes proportional to the evidence.

Let the emotional experience change. Surprise can lead to amusement, then concern, then recognition. Quiet reactions can matter more than another spectacular event. Avoid making participants behave foolishly simply to make the plot work.

Use real actions, objects and constraints. Tell directly when that is the easiest way to orient the listener; show when behavior communicates more economically. Do not confuse “show, don't tell” with refusing to explain essential context.

Resolve the local question. The ending should grow from the events and leave one clear implication. Cut competing morals, repeated plot summaries and obligatory cliffhangers. Further episodes may deepen a skill without making this one incomplete.

## Coherence and discoveries
Primary narrative-memory studies in [the evidence review](../../RESEARCH/short-form-scriptwriting-2026-10-14.md) support the value of relationships among events. Translate that into an outline where each scene changes the situation and helps explain another scene or the ending. Do not invent causation or insert linking words as a cosmetic fix. Each discovery should reward attention and leave a comprehensible next development; this is a house craft heuristic, not a dopamine schedule. Curiosity may impair recall of unrelated material, so keep psychological detail attached to the event that earns it.
