# Business model and opportunity rules

## What we are building

A studio/portfolio, not a personal-influencer business: faceless shows that acquire attention cheaply, build repeat viewership and trust, then introduce relevant high-margin digital products, experiences or communities. Eventually centralize research, writing, asset libraries, publishing and analytics so brands can share infrastructure without looking identical.

**Stages:** find compelling repeatable attention -> show relevant buying demand -> produce efficiently -> improve buyer economics -> build owned reach -> delegate production -> launch another brand.

## What we are optimizing for

A real business, not views for their own sake. Keep five distinct lenses:

1. **Audience pull:** Are there strong existing desires, emotional identity, conflict, curiosity, or entertainment value?
2. **Real buyer:** Which segment has money, a specific desire and a natural reason to purchase? Beware viewer/buyer mismatch.
3. **Digital-first paid precedent:** Prioritize downloadable products, digital games, courses, paid communities, subscriptions and apps. A college/in-person course price is weak evidence for a faceless digital product.
4. **Creative fit:** Can we generate many distinct compelling stories with stylized/cartoon/faceless production?
5. **Founder advantage:** A strong interest enhances taste, persistence and understanding at launch. Apply after establishing commercial plausibility; do not use arbitrary weighted ranks.

Separate **a listed price**, **a membership count**, **actual paid-member count**, and **verified revenue**. None alone establishes our achievable conversion.

## Economics: measure, don't invent niche-specific conversion assumptions

Revenue = qualified video views x external visit rate x purchase conversion x average order value.

Early dashboard: videos published; founder minutes per approved video; per-video tool cost; hook hold/watch time/retention; shares; follows per view; profile visits. After adding real offers: external visits; purchasers; refunds; gross profit per thousand views; customer acquisition cost if paying for distribution; repeat purchases/customer value.

A first offer should be a small honest test of buyer pull, not a giant upfront course or speculative community. Don't build a community whose promise requires constant live work we cannot fulfill in eight hours.

## Current three test accounts

- **Psychology / Power / Influence:** understand how people are influenced or become socially sharper. The audience who likes exposing deception may differ from the audience who pays to improve negotiation/communication. Test bridges separately.
- **Survival / Preparedness:** competence and responsibility under pressure. Buyer may want tailored household readiness rather than generic free safety information. Require factual accuracy.
- **Christian Character / Virtue:** men and fathers seeking grounded faith, courage, duty, self-control and strong example; fatherhood teaching and adult formation are distinct potential customers. Avoid assuming red-pill and diverse Christian denominations form one uniform audience.

Other contenders remain *in reserve*: interactive mystery games, historical/propaganda investigations, activity packs for parents, spirituality/personal transformation, fantasy RPG content and more. They are neither validated winners nor permanently rejected.

## Kill / iterate / concentrate

Early bad data may be platform noise. Review patterns across more than one execution; don't crown a winner on one spike. Explicitly identify what was tested: premise, format, hook, audience, visual style or distribution. Double down on repeatable evidence, not founder affection. Once a format wins, recycle production infrastructure and diversify actual scenes and stories, not nouns in an identical template.
