# Audience Reality First: reusable research method

**Purpose:** Research the whole human story beneath what people say they want. Human beings carry contradictory motives, not just an interchangeable pain point + aspiration.

## Questions before concept development

1. **Worldview and identity:** Who am I? What do I believe is happening? Who is 'us'?
2. **Desire and aspiration:** What do I want to become, experience, protect, restore, master or belong to?
3. **Wound / shadow:** What do I feel I lost? What angers, scares, humiliates or shames me? What hypocrisy do I privately notice? What longing or envy do I rarely admit?
4. **Perceived antagonist:** Who or what do I blame (individual actor, bad incentive, institution, habit, ideology, behavior, system)? What concrete incident supports my view? What parts are interpretation or contested?
5. **Moment of recognition:** What specific 15-second situation feels painfully or delightfully familiar to me?
6. **Cost of inaction:** What will happen if nothing changes? What do I fear my partner, friends or children will think of me?
7. **Agency / dream:** What would a real step forward look like? Which outcome would be exciting enough to pay for?
8. **Buyer fit:** Am I the viewer, buyer or both? What competing free or paid solutions do I already use?
9. **Speech:** Exact *authentic* phrases, cultural references, routines, family roles, objects, settings and idioms naturally used by this audience.

## Research collection

- Use real interview transcripts, comments, forums, book reviews, community descriptions and examples of digital buyers. Distinguish selected anecdotes from representative evidence.
- Keep a short bank of unpolished audience expressions, not synthetic 'viral quotes'. Mark source/context and don't invent verbatim quotations.
- Record positive and uncomfortable motives: belonging, pleasure, desire, insecurity, nostalgia, fear, anger, resentment, competence, status, faith, responsibility, pride, duty, love, humor, grief.
- Blair Warren's famous persuasion categories (encourage dreams; justify failures; allay fears; confirm suspicions; help cast anger at perceived enemies) are useful **diagnostic lenses**, not permission to flatter, lie, inflame or scapegoat.
- An audience's subjective experience deserves faithful depiction. Their causal explanation still has to be treated as a claim, not automatically asserted as objective fact.
- Real conflict and real antagonists make strong stories. Avoid inventing crimes, demonizing whole identity groups or using one person's worst behavior to condemn everyone like them.

## Output: a one-page audience dossier

**ICP:** age/life stage/household/affordability only if useful, plus core job.
**Inner monologue:** 4-6 sentences in the audience's likely voice; explicitly labeled synthetic hypothesis unless sourced.
**Values / losses / fears / anger / ambition:** 2-3 specific points each.
**Actual situations:** 5 concrete social or physical scenes they recognize.
**Antagonists / pressures:** names, incentives or behaviors, with evidence status.
**Paid continuation:** product category + buyer rationale + documented digital precedent.
**Story engines:** 2-3 distinct ways to dramatize the audience's conflict.
**Research gaps:** what we'd have to learn before scripting or building an offer.

## Audience hypotheses (NOT fixed demographics)

**Psychology:** Two motivations worth testing: (a) 'I don't want to be manipulated, duped or publicly embarrassed'; (b) 'I want to read the room and influence people better'. Exposure of scams can capture broad viewers; the buyer for practical influence training may be narrower. Both enjoy realizing the hidden rule behind a scene. Risk: sensational fake body-language/dark-psychology claims.

**Survival (working direction updated 2026-10-15):** Start with people responsible for a household who feel underprepared and do not know what to prioritize. Blackouts make system dependence concrete. Explore responsibility, independence, affordability, fear of being caught unprepared, frustration with scattered advice and pride in being dependable. Desired movement: vague concern → practical priorities → justified confidence and a usable plan. These are audience hypotheses, not sourced interview findings. Country, housing, household composition and buyer demand remain unresolved; the high-rise pilot does not establish the account's whole audience. Distinguish people who enjoy alarming energy/conspiracy content from people who want preparedness help. Research actual situations, authentic language and willingness to act/pay before narrowing the segment or building an offer. See the [account brief](../SHOWS/survival/README.md).

**Christian:** One segment is a Christian father who feels the loss of reliable moral exemplars, fears failing his children, and longs for the kind of courage/faith his family will respect. Another is an adult Christian man seeking his own formation. Anger at cultural change, nostalgia and identity can be sincere, but no specific political blame story is presumed factual. Themes include faith, sacrifice, brotherhood, responsibility, doubt, forgiveness and courage. Risk: cheesy preaching, historical hagiography, denomination blur and unearned authority.
