# Short-form scriptwriting: evidence, limits and a working process
**Research date:** 2026-10-14. **Application:** Rabbit Hole Studios, especially Psychology / Power / Influence and The Setup. **Status:** Research-informed working guidance; no performance guarantee, audience validation or script approval.

## Finding that changes our approach
The strongest starting approach for this account is a clear, entertaining investigation of a real influence mechanism, ending with one judgment the viewer can use. Give viewers enough orientation to understand a specific unanswered question, connect events so each development means something, and reward attention with discoveries along the way. That recommendation combines evidence and editorial judgment; no study establishes it as the universally best Instagram format.

A script can attract attention yet confuse the viewer, be remembered without being shared, or be shared without generating buyer demand. Our process must distinguish those outcomes. A good account episode earns both attention and the account's promised reward: becoming more discerning about credibility and influence. The commercial continuation remains a hypothesis to test.

## Scope and method
Opened official platform documents and primary scientific papers or their primary abstracts. Research covered curiosity, anticipation, suspense, narrative coherence, multimedia distraction, social transmission, social influence and platform measurement. Sources were investigated in parallel, then reconciled with the existing account briefs and writing framework. The table states the studied setting so laboratory findings are not relabeled as organic Instagram experiments.

This is a targeted evidence review, not an exhaustive systematic review or meta-analysis of all social video. Some findings were available through primary abstracts; full-text papers were used where available. Platform announcements describe features and practices, often without public causal evidence. Advice below explicitly identifies our inference where the source does not test a scriptwriting intervention.

## Scientific evidence
| Source | Actual finding and setting | Warranted application / transfer limit |
|---|---|---|
| [Kang et al., 2009, curiosity and learning](https://www.cmu.edu/dietrich/sds/docs/loewenstein/WickCandleLearning.pdf) | Three small student experiments studied trivia, answer seeking and later memory. Curiosity related to reward-associated brain activity and information seeking; intermediate confidence was especially interesting. | Give enough context to understand what is missing. These tasks do not prescribe a hook duration, optimum uncertainty percentage or dopamine dose. |
| [Gruber et al., 2014, curiosity and memory](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4252494/) | Human trivia experiments linked high curiosity with answer memory, incidental face memory and activity/connectivity in reward and memory regions. | An interesting question may help learning its answer. fMRI signals are not direct dopamine-release measurements; this does not establish maximal-dopamine narration. |
| [Brod & Breitwieser, 2019, prediction and curiosity](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41539-019-0056-y) | A preregistered learning experiment with 29 analyzed participants found that making numerical predictions stimulated curiosity compared with generating examples. | A clear situation may naturally invite a prediction. We infer a potential creative use; “guess now” prompts in Reels were not tested. |
| [Bezdek et al., 2017, suspense during film viewing](https://control.gatech.edu/wp-content/uploads/pubs/Bezdek-et-al-2017-Bio-Psych.pdf) | Small naturalistic film/fMRI study linked suspense to attention-related changes and better recall of high-suspense moments. Music versions did not significantly change memory. | Make the possible outcome understandable and consequential. Film/scanner results do not justify a new cliffhanger every few seconds or compulsory dramatic music. |
| [Lee & Chen, 2022, narrative event networks](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-022-31965-2) | Film-event connectivity predicted recall, with a small imaging study and a larger preregistered online replication. Causal and semantic connections both mattered. | Preserve relationships among events and the ending. This supports a coherence rationale; it does not prove adding the word “because” increases Instagram watch time. |
| [Cohn-Sheehy et al., 2021/2022, coherent narratives](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33904017/) | Three fictional-story experiments found better recall for distant events that formed a coherent narrative than events from unrelated narratives. | Connect details across the story instead of stacking isolated facts. Memory for written/experimental stories is distinct from choosing to keep watching a Reel. |
| [Moreno & Mayer, 2000, multimedia coherence](https://tecfa.unige.ch/tecfa/teaching/methodo/Moreno_Mayer00.pdf) | Two instructional-animation experiments with 75 students each found worse learning with added music; environmental-sound effects varied. | Test whether audio and decorative material compete with the explanation. The inference is to protect intelligibility, not prohibit music or entertaining sound in all videos. |
| [Sundararajan & Adesope, 2020, seductive-details meta-analysis](https://rex.libraries.wsu.edu/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/Keep-it-Coherent-A-Meta-Analysis-of/99900601155501842) | Aggregated instructional research found that interesting but irrelevant details can impair learning, with variation by conditions and outcomes. | Cut attractive details without a story or audience-value job. Educational results do not mean comedy or all nonessential visual detail is harmful to entertainment. |
| [Berger & Milkman, 2012, online sharing](https://doi.org/10.1509/jmr.10.0353) | New York Times emailing data plus experiments linked emotional arousal and practical utility with sharing; positive/negative valence alone did not explain the pattern. | Amusement, surprise and useful recognition can supply a reason to share. Email/news and laboratory intentions do not identify the best Instagram hook or predict purchases. |
| [Keller et al., 2024, curiosity counterevidence](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41539-024-00234-w) | Across three task versions with 239 analyzed adults, high curiosity about trivia impaired memory for nearby unrelated scholastic facts. | Keep the learning reward related to the active story question. Curiosity does not automatically improve all information presented alongside it. |
| [Salganik, Dodds & Watts, 2006, social influence and unpredictability](https://www.princeton.edu/~mjs3/salganik_dodds_watts06_full.pdf) | Randomized artificial music-market experiments with 14,341 participants found that social influence increased inequality and unpredictability of outcomes. | Repeat account tests and acknowledge distribution/social-feedback effects. This experiment does not prove what caused any Shed guest's response or any Reel's performance. |

## More direct digital-content evidence and exclusions
A supplementary targeted search examined direct online reading and short-video studies, so the recommendation does not depend only on neuroscience analogies.

- [Berger, Moe & Schweidel, 2023, *What Holds Attention?*](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/00222429231152880): over 600,000 reading sessions across over 35,000 articles plus controlled experiments. Easier processing and emotional uncertainty/arousal were related to sustained consumption. This gives a more direct rationale for familiar, concrete wording and clear syntax. It still studies reading, not narrated organic Instagram; attracting attention and holding it are distinguished.
- [Zannettou et al., CHI 2024, TikTok data donations](https://arxiv.org/html/2301.04945v2): 347 users donated about 9.2 million recommendations. Real use reveals audience/following context and viewing behavior that cannot be reduced to one creative rule. This is observational platform evidence, not a randomized script intervention. Its attention measure is not a universal target or a dopamine measure.
- [Qu & Zhou, 2026, tourism short-video narrative study](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0261517726000506): publisher abstract/snippets describe multimodal observational modeling and multiple configurations associated with sustained viewing. Full methods were not available through the access used, so no effect size, threshold, optimum or mandatory rule is adopted. Sector/platform/context differ from this account.
- [Sheynin, 2025, teenage TikTok attention study](https://www.theyoungresearcher.com/papers/sheynin.pdf): primary abstract reports 100 sampled videos and a 438-response comparison of two videos with bundled characteristics. Its recommendation for fast pacing/music should not be treated as an isolated causal effect of each feature or adult Instagram guidance. Full publisher PDF retrieval failed; the work is not used as a mandatory rule.

These sources reinforce the need to examine actual behavior and scope. Studies comparing bundles of video features cannot tell us which single script sentence caused a result. Newer publication date alone does not make a source stronger. Studies of live streaming, health-message intentions or teenage preferences were excluded from the core recommendation where their setting did not answer the writing question.

## What the evidence does not establish
No reviewed source supplies a script that maximizes dopamine, a compulsory new “hit” every three seconds, an eight-second universal attention span, a fixed best video length, or guaranteed virality. Reward-region activity cannot be translated into a neurotransmitter dose. Neuroscience terminology is unnecessary in our narration unless it is itself the sourced subject.

We also cannot infer that maximum completion, maximum sharing and maximum buyer demand occur with the same script. A shorter video can improve completion while delivering less of the promised learning. A replay can signal confusion. A successful post can owe something to audience composition, distribution, social feedback or timing. Platform weights and exposure tiers were not verified and are not adopted as rules.

## Research-to-writing decisions
These are house procedures informed by the sources; they are not findings directly tested as one package.

1. **Set two outcomes before writing:** why the viewer will stay, and what the viewer will understand or be able to question afterward. For The Setup: entertaining revelation plus better credibility judgment.
2. **Define one intelligible question.** Orient immediately. Preserve interest about the answer without making the situation itself hard to understand.
3. **Build the complete causal outline.** Record the documented inputs, changing conditions and final payoff. Specify where a relation is interpretation rather than evidence.
4. **Consider materially different hooks.** For this case: impossible result; reality-versus-reputation dinner; viewer's usual trust check. Choose a working hook by clarity, relevance and truthfulness; audience preference remains unknown.
5. **Give discoveries during the story.** Each reveal should answer something and alter the situation. This is a craft heuristic, not a timer-based requirement.
6. **Draft connected spoken language.** Name subjects, retain useful transitions and keep each thought easy to follow. A sentence may be longer when its clauses clarify a single relationship.
7. **Place the insight where the events earn it.** Avoid unrelated psychology labels or detours. One brief synthesis can make the mechanism explicit; avoid explaining it fully in the middle and again at the end.
8. **Plan evidence visuals with the narration.** Every asset should clarify, establish atmosphere intentionally or reveal relevant information. Test competing sounds and captions rather than assuming more stimulation helps.
9. **Critique the actual script.** Check comprehension, interest, emotional movement and usefulness separately. An independent model edit improves review diversity but does not replace a human listener.
10. **Measure in stages.** A real voice take and first-listen retelling precede final production; approved audience tests inspect attention, satisfaction and commercial interest separately. See [the testing process](../STUDIO/writing/testing.md).

## Proposed account format
For The Setup, use sourced mini-investigations: a specific credible-looking situation → how the appearance was constructed → a consequence involving real people → a reveal → one question viewers can apply. This fits the current discernment promise while allowing different stories and emotional shapes. It is a recommendation to test, not a universal template imposed on every account.

The Shed is useful because review/rank/refusal/actor cues can be shown rather than merely named. The insight is dependence among credibility signals, not “everyone is gullible.” Free dinner and mixed reactions prevent a false claim that staged popularity proves willingness to pay.

## Research gaps and next evidence
We have not conducted new interviews, measured this account's retention, chosen a narrator through testing, or validated a paid offer. We have not found a peer-reviewed comparison proving the optimum organic Instagram script for this exact audience. The next informative steps are a real one-listen comprehension check, a small set of approved hook explorations and repeated account evidence. User approval gates still apply.
